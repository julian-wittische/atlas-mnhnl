## Description

Large and furry hoverfly with a bright bronze-golden abdomen and long black antennae that are white at the tip.

## Habitat
### Immature

Wet cavities (e.g. rot holes) of old and senescent trees and tree stumps. Larvae are saproxylic.

### Mature

Early flyers can be found on Rosaceae flowers in wooded settings, and sometimes in more natural parks and gardens. Adults can be found resting high up in trees. Dense forests are not necessary but wooded areas with old over-mature trees are.

## Distribution

Always scarce. Most observations in temperate Western Europe.
## Notes

Late-flying individuals can be confused with C. spinolae as they also visit ivy, which one of the only flower left, at this time. C. aurata does not have black bands across tergites 2 and 3.
